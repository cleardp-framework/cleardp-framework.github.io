Abstract

Safe action generation needs explicit clearance

Bimanual manipulation in cluttered environments requires two arms to coordinate while avoiding collisions with surrounding objects. Although diffusion policies have achieved impressive performance in bimanual robot imitation learning, obstacle awareness is typically represented only implicitly through visual or point-cloud encoders. This paper proposes CLEAR-DP, a clearance-guided diffusion policy for obstacle-aware bimanual manipulation. The method segments task objects and obstacles from point-cloud observations and computes geometric clearance between nearby obstacles and the robot's hands or any grasped object. During inference, these clearance measurements define a safety cost for guided sampling. The gradient of this cost is applied only during a few final denoising steps, locally steering the predicted action trajectories away from obstacles while preserving the task-consistent behavior learned from demonstrations. In CLEAR-DP, the guidance is applied to a pretrained diffusion policy, and does not require modifying the policy inputs, changing the training objective, or retraining the policy for new obstacle configurations. The approach is evaluated on obstacle-aware bimanual manipulation tasks on a dual-UR5 setup in RoboTwin, humanoid in Isaac Sim, and on a Astribot robot platform, showcasing improved safety and robustness in static- and dynamic-obstacle scenarios.

Method

Guide only the unsafe part of the trajectory

Overview of the proposed safety-guided diffusion framework for obstacle-aware bimanual manipulation. The framework segments the observed RGB-D scene and computes the clearance between the robot, manipulated object, and nearby obstacles. The resulting distance activates safety gating and, when necessary, guides the final diffusion denoising steps to generate a safer action trajectory.

Evaluation 01

Dual UR5 in RoboTwin

Results are evaluated in both static and dynamic obstacle environments. In the static setting, obstacles remain fixed while their scale and position are varied within the task workspace to test robustness to changed clearance conditions. In the dynamic setting, obstacles move through a bounded region near the robot’s nominal path, testing whether inference-time safety guidance can respond to changing obstacles while preserving task feasibility.

Evaluation 02

Humanoid in Isaac Sim

Similar to RoboTwin, we consider static obstacle configurations in which the obstacle remains fixed during each rollout, while its pose and scale are varied across trials to evaluate robustness to changes in obstacle placement and geometry. In humanoid simulation, we consider a dynamic-obstacle setting in which the obstacle is absent at the beginning of the rollout but suddenly appears during evaluation.
